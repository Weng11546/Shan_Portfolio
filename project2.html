Project overview

This case study uses a visit-level dataset recorded in an Excel workbook. Each of the 50 visits includes the customer type, age, computer unit, usage hours, gaming use, pages printed, food orders, and sales from internet and other services. The workbook summarizes the data with formulas and presents the results in three charts.

Problem statement

The café needs to know which customer types and services bring in the most sales, and which customer habits are linked to higher spending, so that pricing, promotions, and service priorities can be based on evidence.

Objectives

  • Summarize visits, sales, and usage across all 50 visits.
  • Compare the Student, Worker, and Other customer types on visits, total sales, and average sales per visit.
  • Measure how gaming, food orders, and usage hours relate to sales.

Target users

  • Café management, who set pricing and service priorities
  • Staff planning promotions and food offerings

Technologies used

  • Microsoft Excel
  • Formulas (AVERAGE, MEDIAN, COUNTIF, SUMIF, CORREL)
  • Summary tables
  • Excel charts

Key features

Visit dataset

50 visits with 12 fields, from customer type and usage hours to food orders and sales.

Summary statistics

Mean, median, minimum, and maximum for usage hours and total sales.

Customer breakdown

Visit count, total sales, and average sales for each customer type.

Charts

Three charts covering the revenue split, customer types, and sales by visit group.

Data model

  • Categorical: Visit ID, Customer type, Gaming, Food order
  • Numerical: Day, Age, Computer unit, Usage hours, Printing pages, Internet sales, Other sales, Total sales
  • Each row is one customer visit, for 50 visits in total.

Workbook structure

  • Case_5: the raw visit data, plus mean, median, minimum, and maximum for usage hours and sales.
  • Pivot: customer type summary, gaming usage, pages printed, revenue breakdown, and sales by visit group.
  • charts: the three charts drawn from the Pivot sheet.

Key findings

  • Students account for the most visits (20) and the largest total sales (3,315).
  • Visits with a food order average 206.43 in sales, compared with 151.53 for visits without one.
  • Usage hours and total sales are strongly linked (r ≈ 0.79), so longer visits tend to bring in more sales.
  • Gaming does not change spending: gaming visits average 166.14, and non-gaming visits average 167.50.
  • Customers printed 226 pages in total.

Recommendations

  • Promote food orders, since visits that include them tend to have higher sales.
  • Encourage longer sessions, since sales rise with usage hours.
  • Do not rely on gaming alone to raise spending. Pair it with other services.

How the results were checked

All summary values are calculated with formulas, so they update when the visit data changes. The totals were cross-checked against each other to make sure the breakdowns match the overall figures.

Usage hours

Visits average 4.9 hours of computer use, with a median of 5 hours. Usage ranges from 1 to 8 hours.

Limitations

  • The dataset has only 50 visits, so the results describe this sample and may not hold for every period.
  • The links between usage, food orders, and sales show association, not cause.
  • The visit groups are formed by row order in the sheet, not by the Day field.

Future improvements

  • Group sales by the Day field to show true daily or weekly trends.
  • Add more visits or months of data to test whether these patterns hold.
